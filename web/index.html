About Dam Storage Metrics

Live Storage (Useful Content): The active volume of water above the lowest outlet / silt level available for municipal drinking, agricultural irrigation, and hydroelectric generation.

Dead Storage: Water stored below the lowest sluice gates that cannot be drained by gravity, reserved for silt accumulation.

Gross Storage: The entire volume of water stored in the reservoir (Live Storage + Dead Storage).

Hydrological Units Conversion

  • 1 MCM (Million Cubic Metres) = 1,000 Million Litres = 100 Crore Litres
  • 1 TMC (Thousand Million Cubic Feet) = 28.317 MCM = 2,831.7 Crore Litres
  • 1 MCM = 0.035315 TMC

Project Overview & Background

Why this platform was developed and the civic problem it addresses

The Maharashtra Dam Water Storage Analytics System is a database-driven civic-tech and data analytics platform designed to make dam water-storage information across Maharashtra accessible, structured, searchable, and understandable.

Maharashtra has numerous dams and reservoirs distributed across diverse administrative divisions, districts, and river basins. Information about their water-storage levels is published periodically through official bulletins by the Water Resources Department (WRD), Government of Maharashtra.

However, accessing historical records, comparing reservoirs across regions, tracking multi-month storage depletion, and analysing seasonal refill trends can be inconvenient when data is distributed across individual daily PDF reports.

This platform directly addresses that challenge by systematically collecting, extracting, validating, storing, and visualizing this vital public hydrological information through a centralised relational database and an interactive web dashboard. It transforms periodic static reports into structured, searchable, and accessible public civic data.

Database Architecture & Normalisation

Engineered on relational principles for data integrity, efficiency, and zero redundancy

At the heart of the platform is a robust SQLite relational database structured strictly adhering to Third Normal Form (3NF). By decoupling static reservoir metadata from dynamic daily observations, the schema prevents update anomalies and minimises storage footprints.

Relational Entity Separation

Normalized tables for divisions, districts, river_basins, dams, dam_daily_logs, and system_audit_logs ensure zero redundant storage of names or geographic hierarchies.

Referential Integrity

Foreign-key constraints enforce structural hierarchy: dams link to districts, districts link to revenue divisions, and daily time-series logs cascade from registered dams.

Query Optimization & Indexing

Composite B-Tree indexes on (dam_id, report_date) and single-column indexes on report_date ensure sub-millisecond lookups across 130,000+ historical rows.

Transactional Atomic Upserts

Data ingestion utilizes SQLite's ON CONFLICT(dam_id, report_date) DO UPDATE mechanism, guaranteeing idempotent runs with zero data loss or duplication.

Materialised Analytics View (vw_dam_daily_analytics)

A pre-joined SQL view computes derived analytical metrics—including remaining capacity to fill (MCM), live storage fill percentage, and year-over-year percentage point differences—without incurring runtime calculation penalties on the client.

From a Course Project to a Public Platform

The story of expansion from an academic DBMS assignment into a live civic resource

This website originally began as a course project for the Database Management System (DBMS) curriculum during my undergraduate studies in Instrumentation and Control Engineering at Vishwakarma Institute of Technology (VIT), Pune.

The initial academic objective was to ground theoretical relational database concepts—such as normalization, entity relationships, views, and integrity constraints—in a practical, real-world application rather than creating an artificial textbook schema.

What started as a focused classroom project gradually evolved into an extensive statewide information infrastructure. The project scope expanded far beyond academic submission requirements to incorporate automated web scraping, historical multi-year archiving, automated CI/CD synchronization, analytical querying, and an editorial, citizen-friendly dashboard.

Today, the platform is intentionally designed to be openly accessible to everyone—not just students or academic evaluators. It aims to serve diverse user communities:

👥

Citizens & Residents

Everyday citizens seeking transparent, accessible understanding of dam storage levels and municipal water security.

🔬

Hydrological Researchers

Academics, environmental analysts, and climate researchers requiring clean, structured time-series datasets for exploratory study.

🎓

Students & Educators

Learners studying database architectures, full-stack engineering, civic-tech, and practical real-world data systems.

🏞️

Water-Resource Enthusiasts

Individuals passionate about Maharashtra's geographical landmarks, river basins, monsoons, and storage dynamics.

💻

Developers & Civic-Tech Builders

Engineers interested in automated data pipelines, schema design, public data curation, and community tech projects.

The overarching vision is simple: to make critical public water-resource information effortless to access, explore, and understand. Although it was born in an academic classroom, this project is built as an enduring public initiative for the people of Maharashtra.
